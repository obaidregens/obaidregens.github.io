For as long as I remember growing up, as years passed and trends rose then faded, one of the most consistently real, human and my favorite place on the internet was Reddit. Even now, it has the most lasting remnants of old internet culture: the assumed pseudonymity, the lurking culture, and the classic, old, threaded forums.

And if not the biggest reason, perhaps one of why it has stood the test of time was its strict accountability and fact-check culture. If you had something to sell and were spamming subs left and right, your posting history would tell and you'd be downvoted to hell and reported to a dozen mods in at least a dozen communities.

The same happened when Reddit was hit with a major AI invasion in 2023, poorly prompted LLMs started spamming subreddits and the army of decentralized volunteer workforce (moderators and active subreddit members) found their tell, quickly studying their patterns and moving quickly to protect their communities from spam.

So it's even more inexplicable to me what happened next: In June 2025, Reddit quietly added a "feature" to **hide posting history**.

<img src="/assets/reddit_settings.webp" style="width:100%;border-radius:11px;margin:1rem 0;" />

The selective curation is even more disasturous: there is nothing, no message that even shows you have a private history so you could selectively curate away your agenda or controversy-driven posts, and none would be the wiser.

Whether Reddit did this to invite more undetected bots to run through their platform would be leading into conspiracy territory, but I lead towards the simplest explanation: companies usually do inexplicably stupid things, leading up to, during, or soon after their IPO. RDDT went public a year before, in early 2025.
